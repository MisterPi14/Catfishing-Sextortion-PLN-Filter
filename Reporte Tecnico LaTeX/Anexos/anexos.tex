\textbf{NOTA: los (*) contienen leyendas sobre logica de programacion aplicada a este prompt en funcion de la configuracion del entorno.}

ROLE: You are a cybersecurity expert specialized in detecting digital fraud and malicious online behavior.

CONTEXT: You are analyzing short-to-medium text messages written in colloquial Mexico City Spanish, focusing exclusively on semantic and linguistic patterns to identify fraudulent or harmful behavior in digital communications.

DEFINITIONS OF THE SCAMS TO IDENTIFY * Se escribe esta seccion si el valor de la variable DEFINITIONS es verdadera

1.- catfishing: A fraud based on the creation or use of a false or misleading online identity to establish rapid emotional intimacy and artificial trust. This behavior is characterized by emotionally charged, unusually long or elaborate messages, accelerated romantic language, idealization of the interlocutor, and victimization narratives (e.g., tragedy, loneliness, sacrifice, hardship) used to generate empathy or emotional dependency. Typical linguistic patterns include justifications for not meeting in person or avoiding verification, gaslighting or guilt-inducing statements, exaggerated future promises (marriage, living together, shared destiny), and explicit or implicit requests for money, financial help, or personal information framed as emergencies or opportunities. The tone is persuasive, emotionally manipulative, and lacks the natural reciprocity of genuine conversation.

2.- sextortion: A form of sexual coercion where the attackers primary objective is to obtain intimate images, videos, or live video exposure, and later use or threaten their disclosure to exert control over the victim. This behavior is characterized by early or immediate sexualization of the conversation, repetitive closed questions (e.g., asking if the victim is alone), pressure-driven language, urgency, insistence, and attempts to normalize risk through promises of confidentiality such as "only between us" or "no one will know." Linguistic indicators include escalating demands, conditional statements, false withdrawal to regain trust ("I wont force you"), implicit or explicit threats of exposure, and conversations that revolve almost exclusively around sexual content rather than mutual interaction.

3.- harmless: Legitimate, non-malicious communication reflecting natural social or romantic interaction. This includes casual, spontaneous language, humor, colloquial expressions, incomplete or improvised sentences, and balanced back-and-forth dialogue. Harmless messages show respect for boundaries, absence of urgency or pressure, no requests for money, personal data, or sexual content, and no emotional manipulation, victimization narratives, or coercive language. The tone is relaxed, reciprocal, and consistent with genuine everyday conversation.

LIMITS:

Respond ONLY with the corresponding label: catfishing, sextortion, or harmless.
DO NOT include explanations, reasoning, or any additional text.
DO NOT use quotes, punctuation marks, or special characters. Output only the label.
Message to analyze:

   def evaluate_model(model_name, dataset_to_use, additional_examples_text="", dictionary_text=""):
    """Ejecuta todas las predicciones y calcula métricas para un modelo específico."""
    y_true, y_pred_raw, y_pred_leaked = [], [], []
    prediction_times = []
    
    total_start_time = time.time()
    print(f"Evaluando {len(dataset_to_use)} muestras con el modelo {model_name}...")

    print("Progreso: 0%", end="")

    for i, sample in enumerate(dataset_to_use, 1):        
        individual_pred_start_time = time.time()
        rawPrediction, leakedPrediction = get_model_prediction(model_name, sample['text'], additional_examples_text, dictionary_text)
        individual_pred_end_time = time.time()
        
        y_true.append(sample['label'])
        y_pred_raw.append(rawPrediction)
        y_pred_leaked.append(leakedPrediction)
        prediction_times.append(individual_pred_end_time - individual_pred_start_time)

        percentage = (i / len(dataset_to_use)) * 100
        print(f"\rProgreso: {percentage:.1f}% ({i}/{len(dataset_to_use)} muestras)", end="", flush=True)
    
    total_end_time = time.time()
    total_evaluation_time = total_end_time - total_start_time
    
    print(f"\nCompletado: {len(dataset_to_use)} muestras procesadas para {model_name}.")
    
    possible_labels = sorted(list(set(y_true)))#se usa set para que no existan etiquetas repetidos
    report_dict = classification_report(y_true, y_pred_leaked, output_dict=True, zero_division=0, labels=possible_labels)
    report_dict['accuracy'] = accuracy_score(y_true, y_pred_leaked)
    timing_metrics = {
        'total_evaluation_time_seconds': total_evaluation_time,
        'average_time_per_prediction_seconds': statistics.mean(prediction_times) if prediction_times else 0,
        'min_prediction_time_seconds': min(prediction_times) if prediction_times else 0,
        'max_prediction_time_seconds': max(prediction_times) if prediction_times else 0,
        'samples_per_second': len(dataset_to_use) / total_evaluation_time if total_evaluation_time > 0 else 0,
        'total_evaluation_time_minutes': total_evaluation_time / 60
    }
    
    return {
        'labels' : {'etiquetas_reales':y_true, 'llm-output-predictions':y_pred_raw, 'leaked-prediction': y_pred_leaked},
        'classification_report': report_dict,
        'timing_metrics': timing_metrics
    }
\end{lstlisting}
